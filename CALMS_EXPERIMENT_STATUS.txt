WHAT THE EXPANDED STUDY FOUND
At the locked US$0.20 episode budget and development-selected 0.5% audit rate:

                    QA success       Mean cost        Mean net value
CALM-S                 66.0%          $0.0209533         $0.1440467
Equal pooling          66.0%          $0.0208891         $0.1441109
Premium/static         66.0%          $0.0099049         $0.1550951

Net value uses a declared $0.25 equivalent reward per correct answer.
The premium worker is Claude Sonnet 5. It was the development-selected baseline.
CALM-S's paired net-value difference from premium was -$0.0110484 per task;
95% stream-bootstrap interval [-$0.0113179, -$0.0107983], over ten disjoint
15-task streams. This is evidence against a utility advantage in this setting.
Most of the loss is forecasting cost ($0.0109842/task); audit cost at the selected
rate adds $0.0000642/task. Only two audits occurred at this low selected rate.
At the same primary budget, all six tested audit rates had 66% QA success.

Workflow completion: CALM-S 7/24; static 9/24; equal 8/24; selected 7/24;
exploration 5/24. These are descriptive results from three streams, not a
confirmatory significance result. Actual upstream worker outputs were used.

Full-candidate Brier: historical predictor 0.246108; equal LLM pool 0.246976.
The count-matched feedback diagnostic revealed 96 labels in each arm at the
fixed 10% rate: random-candidate Brier 0.246289, selected-candidate 0.246551.
That small descriptive difference does not establish an adaptive utility gain.

Collection had zero invalid forecasts and six incomplete worker outputs; those
incomplete outputs remain failures in the denominators and costs. Workflow
execution had zero invalid forecasts and zero incomplete worker outputs.
Zero shared-budget violations were found in the evaluated runs.

WHAT THIS MEANS FOR THE PAPER
- Do not claim broad practical performance gains or that audits pay for themselves
  in stationary QA. Preserve the negative comparison with equal pooling and the
  strong static worker; beating the cheapest worker is not the audit contribution.
- The mechanism/incentive argument and practical routing benefit remain separate.
  No financial score transfers were implemented in the live runs.
- Do not call this a complete AAMAS study or an externally registered experiment.
  The extension was prospectively fixed after inspecting the first pilot.
- Do not treat the change from first-pilot accuracy to expanded-study accuracy as
  a causal treatment effect: the tasks, hop mix, context formatting and cards differ.
- Report the strong effect of pessimistic audit reserves at the tight budget.
  Rare audits still require reserving the maximum possible audit expense.
- Replace unsupported manuscript claims about a supplied complete preregistration
  or historical simulator reproduction with the actual artifacts and limitations.

HIGH-VALUE REMAINING WORK
