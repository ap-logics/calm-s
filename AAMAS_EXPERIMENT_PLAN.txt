CALM-S: EXPERIMENTS TO PRIORITIZE FOR AAMAS 2027
Prepared 17 September 2026

Purpose: a concrete setup brief, ordered by likely scientific value for review.
This is a proposed study plan, not a completed preregistration or a guarantee
of acceptance. Sample counts below are planning targets unless stated otherwise.

Read: paper/main.tex, paper/references.bib, results/summary.csv,
results/strategic_results.csv, and the structure/budget fields of
results/episode_results.csv. Checked the official AAMAS 2027 main-track call
and primary repositories for the proposed benchmarks. No experiments were run.

1. THE DECISION THAT SHOULD DRIVE THE EXPERIMENTS
================================================

The paper needs to establish the value of audited forecast settlement, beyond
the value of having good independent forecasts. The existing simulation does
not yet establish this:

  Independent equal pool:          14.4268 mean net value
  Selected-only weighted pool:     14.4046
  CALM-S with two audits/episode:  14.1825
  Static router:                   12.8507

CALM-S trails equal pooling by 0.2444 value units, approximately 1.69% of the
equal-pool value. Its mean audit cost is 0.1970. These are descriptive differences;
the supplied marginal confidence intervals are not paired-difference tests.
Beating the static router therefore cannot establish the audit contribution.

My priority is to answer four questions convincingly:
  A. Can realistic independent forecasts improve real-agent allocation?
  B. When does unbiased audit feedback improve decisions enough to pay for itself?
  C. Does the settlement design remove profitable report manipulation under its
     stated assumptions, and where do those assumptions break?
  D. Does this work on workflows with actual dependencies and a shared budget?

Run the core experiments below before adding many benchmark families. A clear
mechanism result, a credible cost frontier, and real workflow evidence are more
valuable here than a large table of weak comparisons. Keep unfavorable results.

Recommended minimum: E0 through E5, plus the core ablations in E6.
E7 is an inexpensive robustness extension if time permits.

3. SHARED HARNESS -- BUILD ONCE FOR ALL EXPERIMENTS
=================================================

Workers and forecasts:
  - Start with four version-pinned workers: one low-cost generalist, one
    intermediate generalist, one stronger generalist, and one specialist or
    different-family worker. Choose exact IDs based on access and pilot evidence
    of complementary ability, not a favorable final-test result.
  - Use three separately configured forecasters, with at least two model families
    where practical. Separate prompts do not establish economic independence;
    define ownership and utility separately from model-error correlation.
  - Each forecaster outputs a probability of passing the declared verifier for
    each eligible worker BEFORE any answer/outcome for that opportunity appears.
  - Inputs: task, available upstream state, worker identity/capability card, tool
    and token limits, and only permitted historical feedback. No hidden tests,
    reference answers, current latent probabilities, or held-out worker outputs.
  - Include a cheap historical empirical predictor and a frozen learned predictor
    trained on development data. This tests whether LLM forecasters earn their
    cost. Use these as baselines, not extra test-set tuning opportunities.
  - Freeze invalid-probability, timeout, retry, and missing-forecast rules before
    testing. Paid failures remain in both cost and success denominators.

Suggested task coverage, in order of setup priority:
  1. Code: HumanEval+/MBPP+ through EvalPlus; use executable tests and pin the
     dataset/evaluator. Useful for the all-worker outcome matrix, not sufficient
     evidence of multiagent workflow performance by itself. [S4]
  2. Multi-hop QA: MuSiQue with fixed supplied evidence or a frozen retrieval
     index; use its answer evaluation and a preregistered binary success rule.
     Exact-match metrics are reproducible but imperfect semantic verifiers. [S5]
  3. Stateful workflow: AppWorld, evaluated in resettable task environments with
     its task-completion checks. This is the preferred real workflow test. [S7]
  4. Optional breadth: a held-out Spider SQL subset using the official distilled
     test-suite evaluator; split by database. Add JSON transformation only if
     a fixed generator and exact correctness checker are already available. [S6]

Splits:
  - Partition by source problem, database, or underlying scenario BEFORE any
    prompt tuning. Keep related paraphrases and subtasks in the same partition.
  - Use separate development/pilot and final evaluation sets. For benchmarks
    with fixed public splits, document any internal split and do not claim an
    official leaderboard result from a custom subset.
  - Freeze task IDs, prompts, model snapshots, seeds, limits, scoring rules,
    evaluation order, hyperparameters, and the analysis plan before final runs.
  - Online adaptation may use earlier revealed test-stream outcomes if explicitly
    part of the policy; it may never use future outcomes or evaluator-only labels.

Money and value:
  - Define principal net value as declared project reward minus all actual
    principal spending. Include worker calls, forecaster calls, audits, tools,
    verification, retries, and any implemented score payments.
  - Distinguish API spending from hypothetical economic transfers. In a social
    welfare analysis internal transfers cancel; in principal profit they do not.
    Publish the two ledgers separately where both interpretations are used.
  - Do not subtract dollars from arbitrary benchmark points without a conversion.
    Freeze a reward-to-currency conversion on development data and report a
    sensitivity curve. Also report success at matched dollar budgets, which
    avoids dependence on an arbitrary reward conversion.
  - Audit fraction r means a fraction of a fixed, predeclared execution-budget
    reference B_exec_ref. Total spending, including audits, must remain below
    B_total. State both denominators and report actual realized audit spending.
  - Tiny audit fractions require randomized audits across opportunities. Do not
    round to zero or exclude expensive candidates without reporting loss of
    positive support. Reserve enough funds for any sampled audit; an infeasible
    positive-support design must be redesigned, not assigned false propensities.
  - The unnormalized score sum in the paper can make payment scale with the number
    of candidates. Freeze beta, P0, and candidate-set size; report expected and
    worst-case payment exposure. Normalize if needed, documenting the change.
    Do not retroactively cap payments while claiming the unchanged theorem.

Common budgets:
  - Pilot a reference cost for running the strongest worker on every project.
    Use tight/medium/loose caps of 25%, 50%, and 100% of that reference where
    feasible. Freeze dollar amounts using development data, not test outcomes.
  - Pick medium as the single primary budget. The other two are sensitivity runs.
  - All baselines use the same workers, tasks, tools, verifier, and spending rules.

4. E1 -- REAL FORECASTS AND SELECTIVE FEEDBACK [CORE; MODERATE API COST]
====================================================================

Question: are the forecasts useful without access to true success probabilities,
and does selected-only observation distort what the router learns?

Setup:
  - Pilot: 30 distinct problems per cheap family, four workers, two independent
    decodes per worker. Use code and QA initially: 60 x 4 x 2 = 480 worker calls.
  - Ask three forecasters for a vector of four worker probabilities per problem
    before execution: 60 x 3 = 180 forecast calls if batched by worker.
  - Main planning target: 150 NEW problems per family, with the same four workers
    and two decodes: 2,400 worker calls and 900 batched forecast calls. Increase
    distinct problems if the pilot power analysis requires it; this is not a
    claim that 300 problems are necessarily sufficient.
  - Store every worker outcome in an evaluator-only outcome matrix. Policies
    receive only the outcomes their protocol would reveal. Full collection is
    research measurement cost, never free feedback for a deployed policy.
  - Use two label-access treatments with identical initial forecasts and update
    rules: selected-only feedback versus report-independent randomized feedback.
    Add a feedback-count-matched comparison to isolate label selection from the
    benefit of receiving more labels. Then report the actual cost-matched result.
  - For sequential replay, retrain/recompute future predictions only from each
    policy's allowed history. Reusing one cached adaptive forecast sequence across
    different feedback histories is invalid. Frozen predictions may be cached.

Comparisons:
  Historical predictor; cheapest predictor; best individual forecaster chosen on
  development data; independent equal pool; selected-only weighted pool;
  CALM-S audit-only weighted pool; full-label learner as a diagnostic ceiling.

Measure:
  Full-candidate and per-family Brier loss, log loss, calibration slope/intercept,
  reliability diagrams, selected-versus-full evaluation discrepancy, worker
  ranking quality, and downstream budgeted value. Predeclare numerical handling
  of 0/1 probabilities for log loss; do not change settlement silently.

Output: one forecast-quality table and a plot contrasting selected-only estimates
with evaluator-only full-candidate estimates.

Interpretation:
  Useful evidence is reduced full-candidate error or better decisions at matched
  information/spending. A better score only on selected tasks is insufficient.
  If forecasts are weak, first diagnose the pilot; do not spend on a large router
  comparison whose essential information source has not been validated.

5. E2 -- END-TO-END UTILITY AND THE AUDIT-COST FRONTIER [HIGHEST PRIORITY]
======================================================================

Question: does CALM-S provide value beyond equal pooling and ordinary exploration,
once all costs and the strongest relevant alternatives are included?

Core policy roster:
  B1 Cheapest feasible worker.
  B2 Strongest single worker, under the same total budget.
  B3 Static cost-aware router fitted on development data.
  B4 Online cost-aware learner with randomized exploration and logged propensity
     correction, using the same total feedback budget. This is essential: audits
     must be distinguished from ordinary exploration. Use a transparent tabular
     or contextual model matching the implemented CALM-S predictor capacity.
  B5 Coupled self-report with winner-only calibration/settlement.
  B6 Independent equal pool, no audits.
  B7 Independent selected-only weighted pool, no audits.
  B8 CALM-S, audit-only updates and a development-selected positive audit rate.
  B9 Agora reproduction, if feasible; closest orchestration comparison. [S2]
  B10 AgentLance reproduction if retaining the market-performance claim. [S3]

Use authors' code/configuration when available and document deviations. A
tabular reputation rule is not AgentLance, and noisy self-confidence is not
Agora. If full reproductions cannot be completed, narrow the claims and label
proxies. Do not spend the last week implementing many low-value weak baselines.

Protocol:
  - Run B1-B8 at the primary budget on both cheap families and the workflow
    suite from E5. Include at least the closest faithful external baseline where
    its task assumptions fit; explain unmatched benchmark coverage explicitly.
  - For B8 sweep r in {0, 0.5, 1, 2, 5, 10}% of B_exec_ref. The r=0 version has
    frozen audit weights and no ongoing audit-settlement guarantee; it is an
    ablation, not a positive-support mechanism.
  - Tune the deployed positive rate only on development data. Report every
    preregistered rate on test data as a frontier, not a best-test-rate headline.
  - Evaluate tight/loose budgets on the core contrasts B4, B6, B7, B8 after the
    medium-budget run. Avoid a full cross product of all experimental factors.
  - Start with stationary honest agents. Repeat the core contrasts under E3
    manipulation and E4 drift, reporting these regimes separately.
  - Offline replay is valid only where cached task-worker outcomes are invariant
    to the policy state. Actual workflows require on-policy execution (E5).

Primary contrast: locked CALM-S versus the strongest non-audited comparator
selected on development data, with equal pooling always shown explicitly.
Also report the exploration comparison separately, whether or not it is strongest.

Primary outcome: paired difference in fully costed episode net value.
Secondary: completion, success at fixed spend, lower-tail value, total spending,
audit/forecast/payment components, p50/p95 wall latency, and budget violations.

Output: main results table plus audit rate versus net value, with paired
uncertainty bands and cost decomposition. Show the zero-audit point.

Decision rule:
  - Broad practical benefit requires a positive held-out advantage against strong
    comparators at common budgets, not merely against a frozen weak router.
  - If audits help only under manipulation/drift, make that the explicit claim
    and quantify the stationary-case insurance cost and the break-even regime.
  - If no tested rate pays for itself, retain the negative result and remove a
    general performance-improvement claim. Do not change rewards until it wins.

6. E3 -- PROFITABLE DEVIATIONS, NOT JUST ONE INFLATION RULE [CORE]
================================================================

Question: does the mechanism make truthful belief reporting optimal under its
assumptions, and how exploitable is it when those assumptions are relaxed?

Part A: controlled known-belief mechanism experiment (cheap simulation).
  - Sweep p in {0.1,0.3,0.5,0.7,0.9}, selection threshold in {0.3,0.5,0.7},
    bonus/score-scale ratio in {0,0.5,1,2}, and audit observation probability in
    {0.01,0.05,0.2,1}. These probabilities are not cost-budget percentages.
  - Search reports q on a 0.01 grid over [0,1], including q=p and the threshold.
    Compare truthful reports, fixed inflation, deflation, threshold targeting,
    and the best report in the searched class. Use exact expected payoffs for
    one-step cases and Monte Carlo to check implemented payments and variance.
  - Compare coupled winner-only scoring; coupled allocation bonus plus independent
    audit scoring; separated audit-only scoring. The middle condition isolates
    why auditing alone does not remove allocation-side interests.
  - Report within-mechanism deviation gain and selection change. Raw payoff
    levels across different payment schemes are not directly comparable.

Part B: strategic policies on real outcomes from E1/E5.
  - Freeze each agent's baseline belief report before applying strategic changes.
    Real LLM truth is unobserved: call this an elicited baseline, not known truth.
  - Optimize deviation parameters on development outcomes, then evaluate on
    held-out tasks. Apply attacks to 0%, 25%, and 50% of eligible participants.
  - Include report-only strategic wrappers first, so manipulation is isolated
    from changes to worker effort or competence. Optional LLM agents receive an
    explicit payoff objective and bounded search budget; failure to find an
    attack is not proof that none exists.
  - Record agent profit/deviation gain, allocation changes, principal net value,
    honest-agent displacement, and forecaster payment variance.

Part C: assumption boundaries (small controlled extension).
  - Add allocation-side utility lambda in {0,0.25,1,4} relative to score scale,
    including a worker-owned forecaster and a colluding pair.
  - Search a two-window deviation: alter reports now to affect future audit-only
    weights, state, selection, and profit. Delayed weights alone do not establish
    a repeated-game equilibrium; future report-dependent exposure matters.
  - Keep lambda=0/theorem-compliant results separate from boundary violations.

Output: deviation-gain table and gain-versus-side-interest curve. Analytic
one-step results should agree with the implementation. Search results are lower
bounds on exploitability unless a global best response is actually solved.

7. E4 -- ADAPTATION WITHOUT LATENT-PROBABILITY LEAKAGE [CORE]
==========================================================

Question: do audits discover changes that selected-only feedback misses?

The existing simulator gives forecasters biased/noisy versions of CURRENT true
probabilities. Its shift result is therefore not evidence that agents learn
unknown changes from sparse feedback. This experiment must remove that shortcut.

Setup:
  - Use frozen real-model responses where replay is valid, or real executions.
    Forecasters initially learn only from the development history.
  - Streams of 80 episodes, a predeclared change at episode 40; use 30 simulator
    seeds for cheap screening. For real streams, size independent task blocks
    using the pilot; resampling cached labels does not create new task evidence.
  - Three conditions: no shift; task-mixture shift from 50/50 to 80/20 between
    two predeclared strata; unannounced worker-capability change (e.g. reduced
    tool/token access for one worker). Mark the latter as an induced stress test.
  - Include an overlooked worker that improves after the change. Initial ranking
    and improvement must be specified before observing final-test outcomes.
  - Update predictions using only allowed past feedback, with one-window delay.
    Repeat key conditions with three-window delay. Do not disclose the changed
    capability matrix or exact change time to the policies.

Compare: B3, B4, B6, B7, B8, with the same initial predictor and total budgets.

Measure: post-change cumulative value loss versus a separately identified
reference, time until the policy changes worker preference, worker coverage,
forecast error, and total audit spend. Report no-shift cost as well.

Output: post-shift learning curves and cumulative value/cost table. If only
synthetic shifts are feasible, label external validity accordingly.

8. E5 -- A REAL DEPENDENT WORKFLOW [CORE; HIGHEST EXECUTION COST]
==============================================================

Question: does the task-DAG orchestration claim survive genuine downstream
dependencies, replanning, and stateful shadow audits?

Preferred setup: AppWorld tasks in resettable environments. Pilot 20 distinct
tasks; plan 80-120 NEW final tasks if available and feasible, subject to power.
Use valid released evaluation splits and keep tuning scenarios separate.

Workflow definition:
  - Freeze a common decomposition/planner for all compared allocation policies.
    Log actual ready nodes, dependencies, observations, failures, and replans.
  - Allocate worker calls at declared decision nodes. Judge success with the
    benchmark's final environment/task checks. Do not award completion merely
    because a language model says all subtasks are complete.
  - A shadow audit forks the exact eligible pre-action state, executes an
    alternative worker in that fork, verifies the local outcome, and discards
    the shadow state. It must not alter the production environment.
  - Define what local binary outcome is forecast. A local action verifier may
    not predict final project success; measure that relationship explicitly.
  - If intermediate states cannot be cloned and verified, audit complete tasks
    from reset and narrow the mechanism claim to that granularity. Do not claim
    unobserved subtask counterfactuals from unrelated standalone executions.

Comparisons: CALM-S DAG/receding horizon; same forecasts/audits with myopic
selection; equal-pool DAG allocator; exploration baseline; strongest single
worker. Add the closest external system where its interface permits fairness.

Measure: final completion, fully costed value, dependency-induced failures,
replans, calls, critical-path latency, and orphaned work with no final value.
On small instances solve allocation exactly to report optimizer gaps; distinguish
estimated-oracle and hindsight upper bounds from a true probability oracle.

Output: workflow results table plus two representative trace diagrams selected
by a declared rule (e.g. median successful and median failed run), not anecdotes
presented as statistical evidence.

If AppWorld setup is impossible in time, implement a versioned executable
code/data workflow with actual upstream outputs and final integration tests.
Label it a constructed workflow; grouping independent questions into a project
does not establish real dependency handling.

9. E6 -- ABLATIONS THAT IDENTIFY THE CONTRIBUTION [CORE; TARGETED]
================================================================

Run on one cheap family plus the workflow suite; use the same forecasts and
budget wherever the comparison permits. Do not cross every knob with every other.

Core ablations:
  A. Equal vs audit-weighted pooling, HOLDING audits and their cost fixed.
     Separately compare equal/no-audit to expose the cost of collecting labels.
  B. Audit-only vs selected-only updates with matched label count and learner;
     additionally show matched total spend. These answer different questions.
  C. Inverse-propensity vs unweighted audit updates under NONUNIFORM auditing.
     Uniform sampling cannot meaningfully establish the need for correction.
  D. Separated vs coupled incentives with the same forecast information quality
     in simulation. Do not confound independence with an artificially better p.
  E. DAG/receding-horizon vs myopic allocator with fixed forecasts and audit policy.
  F. Equal pooling vs exponential weights vs stacking vs Bayesian pooling, fitted
     only on allowed data. The draft explicitly promises these pooling ablations.
     Define the Bayesian model and the stacking learner before final evaluation.

Lower-priority extensions:
  G. One vs three forecasters, including correlated/same-family forecasters.
  H. Frozen vs delayed online weights; show feedback lag sensitivity.
  I. Cheap historical/learned forecasts vs LLM forecasts including inference cost.

Output: compact component table, with incremental value and cost. It is acceptable
for equal pooling to win; revise the weighting claim rather than hide the result.

10. E7 -- PROPENSITY VARIANCE AND SETTLEMENT RELIABILITY [OPTIONAL]
================================================================

Question: is the formally unbiased score operationally usable at sparse audits?

Use the E1 full-outcome matrix and a fixed report set. Sample audits repeatedly
under uniform and predeclared worker-stratified policies, all with positive
inclusion probabilities. For nonuniform sampling use weight ratios 1:2:4:8 over
the four workers as one transparent stress case, with correct marginal inclusion
probabilities for the actual sampling procedure.

Compare unclipped Horvitz-Thompson, clipped weights at 10/50/100, and optionally
self-normalized estimates. Mark clipped/self-normalized variants as generally
biased, and do not transfer the original truthfulness guarantee without analysis.

Report estimator bias/RMSE against the fixed full-matrix score, coverage, effective
sample size, max inverse weight, payment variance and high payment quantiles,
forecaster-ranking error, and routing value. Repeat across independently sampled
outcome matrices to distinguish audit noise from model-outcome uncertainty.

Output: bias/variance/payment frontier. Also test repeated-game and outcome-shift
boundaries from E3; these are not addressed by a one-shot proper-score identity.

11. STATISTICAL PLAN -- FREEZE BEFORE THE MAIN RUN
=================================================

  - Primary H1: paired mean fully costed value improvement of locked CALM-S
    against the development-selected strongest non-audited baseline at the
    medium budget. Equal pooling and the exploration baseline remain visible.
  - Define three secondary hypotheses before looking: reduction in deviation
    gain in E3; improved full-candidate forecast error in E1; and improved
    post-shift cumulative value in E4. Apply Holm correction to these three.
    Treat broad rate/budget/benchmark sweeps as estimation/exploration unless
    a larger multiplicity plan is explicitly preregistered.
  - Preserve paired tasks across policies. For independent problems, bootstrap
    at the original-problem cluster level and stratify by family. Repeated
    decodes stay within their problem; they are not independent samples.
  - For adaptive streams bootstrap independent whole streams or appropriate
    predeclared blocks; an iid episode bootstrap destroys temporal dependence.
    Reused tasks across streams require clustering by original task as well.
  - Equal-weight family aggregates should be declared in advance; show each
    family's results so aggregate wins cannot hide regressions.
  - Pilot power: choose smallest worthwhile effect delta on the declared value
    scale. If using the draft's 5% of oracle value, freeze a positive development
    oracle reference; never use a test-selected oracle or unstable near-zero one.
    For paired independent units, an initial normal approximation is
      n ~= ((1.96 + 1.282) * SD_paired_difference / delta)^2.
    Example: SD=0.25, delta=0.05 gives about 263 independent paired units.
    This is illustrative, not an observed variance. Refine for clustering and
    stream dependence by simulation and predeclare final n and stopping rules.
  - Use 95% paired confidence intervals and effect sizes. A nonsignificant
    difference does not establish equivalence. No repeated peeking until p<0.05.
  - True pseudo-regret is available in the known-probability simulator. For real
    LLMs use held-out estimated probabilities with uncertainty, or label an
    empirical/hindsight gap correctly. Two decodes do not reveal true p.
  - Keep all valid seeded runs; publish exclusions, failed calls, retry charges,
    provider incidents, and missingness. Report worst-decile results with enough
    independent cases to make them meaningful, or label them exploratory.

14. WHAT THE FINISHED PAPER SHOULD SHOW
======================================

Main text, in priority order:
  1. Fully costed real-model results with equal pooling and exploration controls.
  2. Audit-rate/value frontier, including unfavorable stationary results.
  3. Deviation-gain evidence, showing the no-side-interest assumption explicitly.
  4. Genuine workflow results and a compact causal/component ablation.
  5. Learning-after-shift curve if supported by permitted feedback only.

Supplement: full protocol, task/model versions, power analysis, pooling variants,
estimator/payment variance, per-family results, all rates, and reproduction steps.

Acceptance-relevant interpretation (my judgment, not conference requirements):
  - Strongest outcome: a reproducible regime where CALM-S reduces manipulation or
    selection bias AND improves fully costed real-workflow value versus strong
    controls, with clear assumptions and failure boundaries.
  - Useful narrower outcome: audited settlement buys strategic protection at a
    measured cost, paying off only when manipulation or drift is sufficiently
    common. Report the break-even conditions honestly.
  - Weak outcome: independent forecasts beat static routing, but audits never
    help and strategy tests only restate the one-shot theorem. That does not
    adequately validate the central CALM-S contribution as currently framed.

No experiment can repair an overstated theory claim. Clarify one-shot versus
repeated-game truthfulness, conditional audit/outcome independence, matching
shadow/production outcome distributions, marginal versus joint DAG success,
and principal spending versus welfare. The draft's simple propositions should
not be advertised as a general truthful agent market or a learned-regret theorem.
